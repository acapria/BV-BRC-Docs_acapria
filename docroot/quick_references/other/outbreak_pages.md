# BV-BRC Outbreak Pages: Overview and Navigation

BV-BRC outbreak pages bring together information and research resources for selected infectious disease outbreaks. These pages provide a starting point for exploring an outbreak, finding associated genomic data, and moving into BV-BRC tools for further investigation.

## 1. Access outbreak pages

1. Visit [BV-BRC](https://www.bv-brc.org/).
2. Find **Outbreak Tracker** in the site navigation or outbreak features on the homepage.
3. Select the outbreak you want to explore.

The available outbreak pages include:

- [Influenza A(H5N1)](https://www.bv-brc.org/outbreaks/H5N1/) — H5N1 influenza outbreak information and associated genomic resources.
- [Mpox](https://www.bv-brc.org/outbreaks/Mpox/) — outbreak information and resources for mpox. **[Verify direct page URL before publication.]**
- [Measles](https://www.bv-brc.org/outbreaks/Measles/) — measles outbreak information and resources.
- [SARS-CoV-2](https://www.bv-brc.org/outbreaks/SARSCoV2/) — SARS-CoV-2 outbreak resources.

> **Documentation note:** The outbreaks featured on BV-BRC may change as new outbreaks emerge and existing pages are updated. Dates in page titles identify the outbreak or reporting period, not necessarily the most recent update.

## 2. Understand the outbreak overview

Start with the **Overview** section, where available, to read background information and understand the outbreak's context. Depending on the outbreak, the page may include links to surveillance resources, genomic data, analysis results, and external public health information.

## 3. Explore available genomic data

Outbreak pages can provide routes into relevant BV-BRC data. Follow the available links to examine genomes, sequences, and associated metadata.

When reviewing results, consider:

- **Organism and subtype or lineage:** Confirm that the records match the pathogen or outbreak of interest.
- **Host and geographic metadata:** Use these fields to narrow results when they are available.
- **Collection and submission dates:** Distinguish when a sample was collected from when its sequence became available.
- **Genome and sequence completeness:** A genome record and its individual sequences may have different completeness characteristics.
- **Data availability:** Public sequence records may not represent every reported case or sample.

For influenza, see the [Influenza Search](https://www.bv-brc.org/) interface and the accompanying Flu Corner guide for detailed search and segment-selection instructions.

## 4. Review outbreak reports and analysis

Some outbreak pages link to reports or other curated analyses. For example, the [H5N1 outbreak reports](https://www.bv-brc.org/docs/reports_and_analysis/H5N1_Weekly_Report.html) summarize publicly available genomic data, metadata patterns, and phylogenetic analyses for the outbreak.

Check each report's date and data coverage before interpreting its findings. A report represents the data available at the time of analysis and may differ from current BV-BRC search results.

## 5. Continue your analysis in BV-BRC

After finding records of interest, use BV-BRC tools to investigate them further:

- **[Multiple Sequence Alignment and SNP Analysis](https://www.bv-brc.org/docs/quick_references/services/msa_snp_variation_service.html):** Compare homologous nucleotide or protein sequences and examine sequence differences.
- **[Viral Genome Tree](https://www.bv-brc.org/docs/quick_references/organisms_taxon/phylogeny.html):** Explore evolutionary relationships among selected viral records, using suitable inputs.
- **[Meta-CATS](https://www.bv-brc.org/docs/tutorial/metacats/metacats.html):** Compare aligned sequences from different groups to identify positions associated with group differences.
- **[BV-BRC Tutorials](https://www.bv-brc.org/docs/tutorial/):** Find additional step-by-step guidance for available services.

For segmented viruses such as influenza, ensure that comparisons use the intended homologous segment sequences.

## 6. Example workflow: Explore H5N1 influenza

1. Open the [H5N1 outbreak page](https://www.bv-brc.org/outbreaks/H5N1/).
2. Read the outbreak overview and identify the reporting period and scope.
3. Follow the page's available genomic data or report links.
4. Inspect influenza records and metadata, including host, location, subtype, and segment availability.
5. Refine your search in BV-BRC if you need a specific host, region, date range, or influenza segment.
6. Download the relevant sequences or metadata, or use an appropriate BV-BRC analysis service.

## 7. Frequently asked questions

**Are outbreak pages a complete record of all infections?**  
No. The genomic data available through BV-BRC depend on public sequence submissions and available metadata. Not every reported case has an associated public genome sequence.

**Are outbreak pages updated in real time?**  
Do not assume so. Review any update dates, report dates, and linked source information to understand how current the information is.

**Can I use outbreak sequences for my own analyses?**  
Where records are available in BV-BRC, you can inspect them and use supported download and analysis workflows, subject to the input requirements of each service.

**How do I find a specific influenza segment?**  
Use Influenza Search and its segment filters; consult the Flu Corner guide for a detailed walkthrough.
